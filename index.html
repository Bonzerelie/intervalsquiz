Welcome! 👋

For this quiz you'll need to correctly identify musical intervals. You will be given a starting note and an interval direction (e.g., Major Third above C), and your task is to find the target note. Fill out the quiz, submit your answers, and see how you did! You can use the virtual piano at the top to work notes out, and the 'Information' button for help, and can change the input method from a dropdown to a virtual keyboard for each question.

You can download a scorecard of your results once you submit your answers, and can also download the quiz as an A4 task sheet to print off or send to students. The number of pages will adjust to the number of questions, and the input method you choose for the quiz will be shown on the task sheet download so you can either write the note name for each question, or colour in the note on a keyboard.

    More information 🧐

    In music, an interval is the distance in pitch between two notes. Being able to quickly identify the target note of an interval is an exceptionally useful skill.

    Intervals are measured in semitones (or half-steps). Here are the intervals between a perfect unison (two identical pitches), up to an octave.

    Perfect Unison (P1) : 0 semitones
    Minor 2nd (semitone / halftone / m2): 1 semitones
    Major 2nd (tone / whole tone / M2): 2 semitones
    Minor 3rd (m3): 3 semitones
    Major 3rd (M3): 4 semitones
    Perfect 4th (P3): 5 semitones
    Tritone (TT): 6 semitones
    Perfect 5th (P5): 7 semitones
    Minor 6th (m6): 8 semitones
    Major 6th (M6): 9 semitones
    Minor 7th (m7): 10 semitones
    Major 7th (M7): 11 semitones
    Perfect Octave (P8): 12 semitones

    Use the reference keyboard at the top to audition notes. You can count the semitones up or down from the starting note to find your answer.
